## 3\. Narration (audible, full \~30 seconds, timed to the scenes above)

"November 15th, 1966. Point Pleasant, West Virginia. \[scenes 1–2\] A young couple driving past an abandoned munitions plant sees something that shouldn't exist — \[scenes 3–4\] red eyes, wings, six feet tall. \[scene 5\] Dozens more witnesses follow over the next thirteen months. \[scenes 6–7\] Then, exactly thirteen months later, the Silver Bridge collapses. \[scenes 8–10\] Forty-six people die. \[scene 10\] Was it a misidentified owl — or a warning nobody heeded? \[scene 11\] The file is still open. Archive \#001: The Mothman. The archive stays open." \[scene 12\]

That's the full VO track — record it yourself as "The Archivist" (or a placeholder TTS voice for a first pass) and lay it over the assembled Seedance clips in CapCut, the same way your existing episodes work. Seedance doesn't generate this narration itself (see why in section 5) — it's added afterward as its own audio track.

## 4\. Documentary style (carried through all 12 scenes)

Photorealistic, not animated — real human actors in accurate period clothing, naturalistic lighting, subtle 16mm grain, the History Channel/Discovery "unsolved mystery" reenactment look. Mothman himself is never fully or clearly shown in any scene — only glimpsed as a dark shape with reflective red eyes, obscured by shadow, darkness, or motion blur. That's both truer to how real cryptid documentaries actually shoot the creature and more reliable to generate — a fully lit "monster" is where these AI models tend to go cartoonish.

## 5\. Ending on the Midnight Archive brand

Scene 12 is the brand beat — reuse your existing frame\_end / case-file flat-lay assets if you want the exact established look, or generate it fresh per the ChatGPT prompt above. It should land on: the case file / wax seal / coded postcard, the on-screen tag "Archive \#001: The Mothman," your sign-off line "The archive stays open," and (added in CapCut, not baked into the Seedance clip) your subscribe CTA.

## 6\. Where to go in Higgsfield — and a correction on the tool you're currently in

**Shorts Studio (the page you linked) is not the right tool for this.** I looked into it directly: Shorts Studio takes footage you already have and restyles it — you upload an existing clip, pick a visual preset, and it re-renders that same footage in a new look while keeping the original motion and timing. It doesn't generate new scenes from images or prompts, which is what building these 12 scenes requires. That's almost certainly why the copy/prompt fields feel disconnected from the storyboard — that page isn't built to take scene-by-scene prompts at all.

**The tool you want is Higgsfield's Cinema Studio** (higgsfield.ai/cinematic-video-generator) — that's the part of Higgsfield built for generating new footage from reference images and prompts, with model selection that includes Seedance 2.0, and a multi-shot mode made for exactly this kind of sequence. One honest caveat: Higgsfield ships new Cinema Studio versions often (2.0/2.5/3.0 are all referenced in their own docs), so a button name below might be phrased slightly differently by the time you look — if something doesn't match exactly, look for the nearest equivalent control (they're describing the same handful of concepts every version) and tell me what you see so I can adjust.

**The constraint that shapes the plan:** Cinema Studio's manual multi-shot mode caps out around 6 shots per project. Twelve scenes means **two Cinema Studio projects, 6 shots each**, stitched together afterward in CapCut — same idea as before, just organized around Higgsfield's actual limit instead of a generic one.

  - **Project A:** Scenes 1–6 (cold open through time-passing)
  - **Project B:** Scenes 7–12 (the bridge through the Archive ending)

For each project:

1.  **Upload your reference images as Elements.** Higgsfield's system wants each reference image *named*, and then called by that exact name in your prompts — so name them plainly and consistently, e.g. Scene1, Scene2, Scene3... through Scene6 (repeat Scene7–Scene12 in Project B). This is also the fix for the "copy" problem: type the matching one-line description from section 2 right into that Element's name/notes field so the text travels with the image instead of getting lost.
2.  **Model:** select **Seedance 2.0** from the model dropdown.
3.  **Shot mode:** choose **manual multi-shot** (not single-shot, not automatic) — manual is what lets you control each of the 6 scenes individually instead of letting it invent its own pacing.
4.  **Per shot:** reference that scene's Element by name (e.g. Scene1), paste its one-line description as the prompt, and set one camera movement per shot (e.g. slow push-in for the cold open, locked-off for the driving shots, slow pull-out for the collapse) — pull the specific camera notes from section 2/section 4 above.
5.  **Duration per shot:** set each shot as short as the tool allows (aim for \~2–3 seconds each; if the minimum is higher, generate slightly long and trim in CapCut) — six shots at that length gets you to roughly 15 seconds per project, matching half of your 30-second target.
6.  **Aspect ratio (sizing):** **9:16** vertical, every project, every shot — this is the one setting to double-check before every generation since it's easy to leave on a default.
7.  **Resolution (sizing):** the lowest/draft resolution option while you're testing the look across all 12 scenes; only bump to the highest resolution once you've picked your final cut, so you're not paying premium rendering on drafts you might redo.
8.  **Audio:** if there's a dialogue/character-voice feature, skip it — that's for on-screen characters speaking with lip-sync, and your narrator is off-screen. Set audio to ambient/real-world sound only, no music, no dialogue. Your narration from section 3 gets recorded separately and laid over everything in CapCut, same as your existing pipeline.
9.  **Generate each of the 6 shots in the project, review, regenerate individually if one doesn't land**, then repeat for the second project.
10. **Assemble in CapCut:** both projects' outputs in order, trimmed to fit the 30-second target, one continuous narration track over the top, captions, and your existing end-card branding to close.

**Before building both full projects:** generate just Project A's first shot (the cold open) and check it actually reads as realistic documentary, not cartoon, before spending the other 11 — same cheap-test logic as before, just pointed at the right tool this time.

*(One extra option worth knowing about, for later: once you have a finished draft, Shorts Studio isn't useless — it's actually a legitimate final-polish step. You could run your assembled 30-second cut back through Shorts Studio afterward to apply a caption/pacing preset on top. Just don't start there.)*
