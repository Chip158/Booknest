# Mothman TikTok Script
> Source: mothmantiktokscript.txt (Google Drive)

MIDNIGHT ARCHIVE — TIKTOK SCRIPT
Case File \#001: The Mothman
Target run time: \~55 seconds

======================================================
HOOK — pick ONE. Say it as the VERY first words. No "hey guys," no intro.
======================================================

1) THE TIMING HOOK (curiosity + stakes — recommended)
 "In 1966, two couples saw something on a dark road they couldn't explain.
 Exactly thirteen months later... it started to make horrible sense."

2) THE 'PUT THEM IN IT' HOOK (second-person immersion)
 "You're driving down an empty road at midnight...
 when a seven-foot creature with glowing red eyes lifts off the ground
 and starts chasing your car. This actually happened."

3) THE 'WAIT, WHAT' HOOK (pattern interrupt)
 "There's a town in West Virginia that throws a festival every year...
 for a monster. Because some people there believe it tried to warn them."

\--\> Whichever you pick, the NEXT line is the retention promise below. Don't skip it.

======================================================
RETENTION PROMISE — say this right after the hook (this is what stops the scroll)
======================================================

"And it's not the creature that makes this story terrifying.
 It's what happened thirteen months later."

======================================================
SCRIPT — read straight through
======================================================

November 15th, 1966. Point Pleasant, West Virginia.

Two young couples are driving past an abandoned World War Two explosives plant... when their headlights catch something standing on the side of the road.

About seven feet tall. Shaped like a man — but with huge folded wings... and two glowing red eyes.

They panic and floor it. And the thing spreads its wings, lifts into the air, and follows them — keeping pace with the car at nearly a hundred miles an hour.

They report it to police that same night.

And over the next thirteen months, dozens of people describe the exact same thing. Massive wings. Red eyes. Completely silent. And this overwhelming feeling of being watched... before they ever saw it.

Soon the newspapers give it a name. The Mothman.

Then — December 15th, 1967. Exactly thirteen months after that first sighting... the Silver Bridge, packed with rush-hour traffic, collapses into the river.

Forty-six people die.

And after that night... the sightings almost completely stop.

======================================================
PAYOFF + ENGAGEMENT — the part that earns comments and follows
======================================================

So what was it?

An unknown animal? Mass panic? ...Or a warning nobody understood until it was too late?

Drop what YOU think it was in the comments.

Because this is only Case File number one in the Midnight Archive —
and next time, we open a file that's even harder to explain.

The archive stays open. \<-- (recurring sign-off — use this exact line on every video so people start to recognize it)

======================================================
WHY THIS WORKS (for you — don't read this)
======================================================
\- COLD OPEN: the hook is the first thing said. No intro = no drop-off in the first 2 seconds.
\- OPEN LOOP: "it's not the creature... it's what happened 13 months later" makes them stay for the payoff.
\- SPECIFICS BUILD TRUST: exact dates, "seven feet," "46 people" — precise numbers feel true and keep attention.
\- THE TWIST LANDS LATE: the bridge is the emotional gut-punch — slow down there, let it breathe.
\- COMMENT BAIT: "drop what you think" drives comments, which is what pushes the video to more people.
\- SERIES HOOK: "Case File \#001" + "next time is even harder to explain" = a reason to follow, not just like.
\- REPEATABLE SIGN-OFF: "The archive stays open." becomes your signature line across every episode.

PACING & B-ROLL CUES (for you — don't read this)
======================================================

Tip: run it through CapCut auto-captions, then delete the on-screen words at the bridge moment so the images hit harder.
Accuracy note: the "100 mph chase" and "red eyes" are straight from the original witness reports; the name "Mothman" came from a newspaper (a play on Batman, which was big on TV in '66).
