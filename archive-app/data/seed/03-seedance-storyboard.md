## Read this first: what your own data says

Your performance analysis (pulled from your real IG/TikTok accounts, Oct 2026) found the produced Mothman video is your **worst-performing format** — 328 views on IG, 77–200 on TikTok — while simple product/founder/offer posts hit 2,650–23,400. The pattern across every account that's winning in this niche (MrBallen, Hunt A Killer): **simple, hook-first, interactive, series-based** beats cinematic production.

That doesn't mean skip this — a sharper visual asset can genuinely help. It means build it so it fights *for* attention in the first 2 seconds and gives people something to do (comment, guess, follow for the decode key), rather than just being pretty. The storyboard below is built with that in mind: a short cold-open hook, a mid-video comment-bait beat, and a cipher/CTA ending — not a wall-to-wall cinematic retelling. Consider cutting **two versions**: a 15–20s hook-only teaser for TikTok, and the fuller \~45–55s cut for IG/Shorts where narrative content already does better for you.

## Visual style guide

*(Paste this at the top of any ChatGPT/image-gen prompt so every frame stays consistent.)*

  - **Palette:** cream \#efe5cd, card \#f4ecd9, tan \#cdbd97, ink \#241f18, red accent \#a2342a.
  - **Mood:** vintage case-file / found-footage noir. Desaturated blues, browns and grays with a single saturated red highlight per frame (usually the eyes, a crack, or a wax seal).
  - **Texture:** 16mm film grain, subtle scanline/VHS artifact, newsprint/typewriter texture on any props (nods to the brand fonts Special Elite + Cormorant Garamond + Oswald without needing real text baked in).
  - **Mothman treatment:** always a **dark silhouette with two glowing red eyes** — never a literal photo-real render, never a copy of the real Mothman Museum statue. Keep it abstract/shadow-shaped; that's the brand's existing brand-safe treatment.
  - **People:** silhouettes, back-of-head, or hands only — no depicted faces of real historical people (the witnesses, Mary Hyre). Keep it evocative, not a reenactment with "faces."
  - **Format:** 9:16 vertical, cinematic aspect crop within frame (leave a little headroom — captions get added in CapCut afterward, don't bake in text).

## Seedance 2.0 prompts (one per animated shot)

Seedance 2.0 responds best to: **\[Subject\] → \[Action\] → \[Environment\] → one primary \[Camera\] move → \[Style\] → \[Constraints to avoid\]**, kept to roughly 60–100 words. Feed each ChatGPT storyboard frame in as the reference image (image-to-video mode) and pair it with the matching prompt below. Generate at \~5s first to confirm the style holds, then extend. Vertical 9:16 doesn't cost more than 16:9, so stay vertical throughout. Mute audio on export in all cases — your VO and music get added in CapCut, so don't let Seedance's default score fight with it.

**Shot 1 — The Hook**

A towering dark humanoid silhouette with folded wings and two glowing red eyes, standing motionless at a forest treeline at night. The eyes glow slightly brighter as thin fog drifts past; otherwise the figure stays still. Setting: pitch-black industrial wasteland at the edge of the woods, thin ground fog, one distant flickering streetlamp, cold blue moonlight. Camera: slow push-in toward the eyes. Style: gritty 16mm grain, desaturated blue/brown noir, saturated red only in the eyes, subtle VHS scanline texture. Avoid: no visible face or mouth detail, no extra limbs, no jitter, no text, no music — ambient wind and distant crickets only.

**Shot 2 — The Site**

Rows of crumbling WWII munitions bunkers half-swallowed by overgrown brush. Wind moves the tall grass and low fog rolls across the ground. Setting: dusk, abandoned wildlife-refuge industrial site, heavy overcast sky, amber sodium-light glow on the horizon. Camera: slow aerial pull-back revealing the scale of the bunker field. Style: same noir grain and desaturated tan/ink palette. Avoid: no people, no modern vehicles or signage, no jitter, no text, ambient wind only, no music.

**Shot 3 — Followed**

Interior point-of-view from the back seat of a 1966 sedan at night, two silhouetted figures in the front seats. The rearview mirror catches a pair of red eyes pacing alongside the car in the dark, then the shape drops back and vanishes. Setting: empty two-lane country road at night, headlights sweeping the pavement. Camera: locked-off dashboard framing, no camera movement. Style: grainy analog texture, warm dashboard glow against cold exterior blue-black. Avoid: no visible creature detail beyond the eyes, no dialogue, no jitter, ambient engine and road noise only, no music.

**Shot 5 — The Bridge**

A steel suspension bridge at rush hour, cars bumper to bumper. The camera holds, then a single eyebar joint begins to hairline-crack with a faint spark of stress. Setting: gray winter afternoon, a river below, riverbank towns on either side. Camera: slow rack focus from the traffic to the cracking eyebar. Style: same noir grain, desaturated except a thin red glow along the crack. Avoid: no visible faces in the cars, no gore, no text overlay, ambient traffic sound only, no music.

**Shot 6 — The Collapse**

The same bridge now torn apart, sections folding and dropping into the river below, water erupting around the falling steel. Setting: dusk, winter, river spray, distant emergency lights beginning to flicker on. Camera: slow pull-out wide shot revealing the full collapse. Style: same grain and palette, a faint red bleed creeping in from the frame edges like a warning. Avoid: no visible people or casualties, no gore, no text overlay, ambient rumble and water only, no music.

**Shot 8 — The Archive**

A desk-top flat-lay: aged case-file paper, a red wax seal, and a Field Kit postcard with a coded text strip. A gloved hand (no face visible) sets a wax stamp down onto the seal, then lifts it away. Setting: warm desk-lamp light, moody shadows, cream/tan tones. Camera: slow orbit around the flat-lay. Style: warm case-file cinematography, subtle typewriter-adjacent texture on the props. Avoid: no visible face, no baked-in text (add captions in edit), ambient paper-rustle only, no music.

## Narration script (\~45–55s, matches your existing VO + CapCut pipeline)

November 15th, 1966. Two young couples are driving past an abandoned munitions plant outside Point Pleasant, West Virginia — and they see something that shouldn't exist. Six to seven feet tall. Wings. Two glowing red eyes. It follows their car at a hundred miles an hour... without ever flapping its wings.

Over the next thirteen months, dozens more people report the same thing.

Then, December 15th, 1967 — rush hour. The Silver Bridge collapses into the Ohio River. Forty-six people die. Exactly thirteen months after the first sighting.

Some say it was a misidentified snowy owl. Others say it was never an animal at all — it was a warning nobody heeded.

What do you think it was? Drop your verdict below.

This case file — the sightings, the timeline, the coded transmission still waiting to be cracked — ships in the Archive. The archive stays open.

Keep the cipher (BKEAFGHEKEQNT) visible on screen at the end but don't solve it in the video — that's the point; the decode key ships with next month's Archive.
